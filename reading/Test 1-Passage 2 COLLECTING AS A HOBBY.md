**Paragraph 1 — Why do people collect?**

Collecting must be one of the most ==varied== of human activities, and it's one that many of us psychologists find fascinating. Many forms of collecting have been ==dignified with a technical name==: an **archtophilist** collects teddy bears, a **philatelist** collects postage stamps, and a **deltiologist** collects postcards. ==Amassing== hundreds or even thousands of postcards, chocolate wrappers or whatever, takes time, energy and money that could surely be ==put to much more productive use==. And yet there are millions of collectors around the world. Why do they do it?

> [!quote] 🇨🇳 中文翻译 — Paragraph 1
> 收藏必定是人类最多样化的活动之一,也是我们许多心理学家觉得着迷的一种活动。许多收藏形式都被赋予了一个专业的名称:archtophilist 收藏泰迪熊,philatelist 收藏邮票,deltiologist 收藏明信片。积攒成百上千,甚至上万张明信片、巧克力包装纸或其他什么东西,要花费本可用于更有成效之处的时间、精力和金钱。然而,全世界仍有数百万的收藏者。他们为什么要这么做呢?

> [!tip] 📝 Notes — Paragraph 1
> **Vocabulary & phrases**
> - **varied** (adj.) = of many different types 多样的、各式各样的。
> - **dignified with a technical name** = given an impressive/formal name 被赋予一个专业(体面)的名称。(*dignify* = 使显得庄重/有尊严)
> - **archtophilist / philatelist / deltiologist** = collectors of teddy bears / stamps / postcards 泰迪熊/邮票/明信片收藏者。(不用记拼写,知道是"某类收藏者"即可)
> - **amass** (v.) = to gather a large quantity of something 大量积累、积攒。
> - **put to productive use** = to use in a useful/worthwhile way 用于有成效的用途。
>
> **Main idea 主旨:** Collecting is hugely varied and consumes time, energy and money — yet millions do it. The author (a psychologist) sets up the passage's central question: **why?** 收藏形式极其多样,耗费时间、精力和金钱,却仍有数百万人乐此不疲。作者(心理学家)由此抛出全文的核心问题:**为什么?**(下文逐一给出动机)

**Paragraph 2 — Reason 1: to make money (an instrumental reason)**

There are the people who collect because they want to make money - this could be called an ==instrumental reason== for collecting; that is, collecting as ==a means to an end==. They'll look for, say, ==antiques== that they can buy cheaply and expect to be able to sell at a profit. But there may well be ==a psychological element==, too - ==buying cheap and selling dear== can give the collector ==a sense of triumph==. And as selling online is so easy, more and more people are joining in.

> [!quote] 🇨🇳 中文翻译 — Paragraph 2
> 有些人收藏是因为想赚钱——这可以称为一种"工具性的"收藏理由;也就是说,把收藏当作达到目的的一种手段。比如,他们会寻找可以低价买进、并期望能获利卖出的古董。但这里很可能也有心理层面的因素——低买高卖能给收藏者带来一种胜利的快感。而且,由于网上销售如此便捷,越来越多的人加入了进来。

> [!tip] 📝 Notes — Paragraph 2
> **Vocabulary & phrases**
> - **instrumental reason** = a reason where the activity is a tool for another goal 工具性的理由(把收藏当工具)。
> - **a means to an end** (idiom) = something done not for itself but to achieve something else 达到目的的手段。⭐ Contrast: an end in itself 目的本身。
> - **antiques** (n.) = valuable old objects 古董。
> - **buy cheaply … sell at a profit** = 低价买入……获利卖出。
> - **buying cheap and selling dear** = 低买高卖。(*dear* here = expensive 昂贵地 — 古用法)
> - **a sense of triumph** = a feeling of victory/success 胜利感、成就感。
> - **a psychological element** = 一种心理因素。
>
> **Main idea 主旨:** The first motive is money — collecting as *a means to an end*. But even here there's a psychological payoff: the *triumph* of buying cheap and selling dear. Online selling has made this easier. 第一个动机是赚钱——收藏是"手段"。但即便如此也有心理回报:低买高卖的"胜利感"。网络销售让这更容易。

**Paragraph 4 — Reason 3: finding something special (and the danger of the hunt)**

Another ==motive== for collecting is ==the desire to find something special==, or a particular example of the collected item, such as ==a rare early recording== by a particular singer. Some may spend their whole lives in ==a hunt== for this. Psychologically, this can ==give a purpose to a life== that otherwise feels ==aimless==. There is a danger, though, that if the individual is ever lucky enough to find what they're looking for, rather than celebrating their success, they may ==feel empty==, now that ==the goal that drove them on== has gone.

> [!quote] 🇨🇳 中文翻译 — Paragraph 4
> 收藏的另一个动机,是渴望找到某件特别的东西,或所收藏物品的某个特定样本,比如某位歌手一张稀有的早期录音。有些人可能会穷尽一生去追寻这样东西。从心理上说,这能为一段原本感觉漫无目的的人生赋予目标。不过这里有一个危险:如果这个人有朝一日真的幸运地找到了他们苦苦寻找的东西,他们非但不会为成功而庆祝,反而可能感到空虚——因为那个曾驱使他们前进的目标,如今已不复存在。

> [!tip] 📝 Notes — Paragraph 4
> **Vocabulary & phrases**
> - **motive** (n.) = a reason for doing something 动机。
> - **the desire to find something special** = 寻找特别之物的渴望。
> - **a rare early recording** = 一张稀有的早期录音。
> - **a hunt** (n.) = a long search 追寻、搜寻。→ *spend their whole lives in a hunt* 穷尽一生去追寻。
> - **give a purpose to a life** = 为人生赋予目标。
> - **aimless** (adj.) = without purpose or direction 漫无目的的。
> - **feel empty** = 感到空虚。
> - **the goal that drove them on** = 驱使他们前进的目标。(*drive sb on* 驱使某人前进)
>
> **⭐ The paradox 悖论:** the *search* gives life meaning, so *succeeding* can backfire — the collector feels **empty** once the driving goal is gone. 追寻本身赋予人生意义,因此"成功"反而可能适得其反——目标一旦达成,收藏者会感到空虚。(IELTS 常考这个"反直觉"的点)
>
> **Main idea 主旨:** The third motive is the thrill of the hunt for a rare item, which gives an aimless life purpose — but finding it can leave the collector feeling empty. 第三个动机是追寻稀有藏品的刺激,它能为漫无目的的人生赋予目标——但一旦找到,反而可能让人感到空虚。

Stamp collecting ==opens a window to other countries==, and to the plants, animals, or famous people shown on their stamps. Without those collections, our understanding would be greatly ==inferior to== what it is.

> **Main idea 主旨:** The fourth motive/benefit is education: stamps teach about other countries; 19th-century natural-history collections built much of our understanding of the natural world. 第四个动机/益处是教育:邮票让人了解他国;19世纪的自然史藏品奠定了我们对自然世界的大量认识。

**Paragraph 6 — Example: trainspotting and expert knowledge**

In the past - and nowadays, too, though ==to a lesser extent== - a popular form of collecting, particularly among boys and men, was ==trainspotting==. This might involve trying to see every ==locomotive== of a particular type, using published data that identifies each one, and ==ticking off== each engine as it is seen. Trainspotters exchange information, these days often by mobile phone, so they can work out where to go to see a particular engine. As ==a by-product==, many ==practitioners== of the hobby become very ==knowledgeable about== railway operations, or the ==technical specifications== of different engine types.

> [!quote] 🇨🇳 中文翻译 — Paragraph 6
> 过去——如今也是,只是程度较轻——一种流行的收藏形式(尤其在男孩和男性中)是"火车观察记录"(trainspotting)。这可能包括:借助能识别每一辆机车的公开资料,努力见到某一特定型号的每一辆机车,并在见到每一台机车时逐一打勾记录。火车迷们会交换信息,如今常常通过手机,以便弄清该去哪里才能看到某一台特定的机车。作为一种副产品,许多从事这一爱好的人变得对铁路运营、或不同机车型号的技术规格非常在行。

> [!tip] 📝 Notes — Paragraph 6
> **Vocabulary & phrases**
> - **to a lesser extent** = to a smaller degree 程度较轻、在较小程度上。
> - **trainspotting** (n.) = the hobby of watching trains and recording their numbers 火车观察记录(爱好)。
> - **locomotive** (n.) = a railway engine 机车、火车头。(= engine 此处同义)
> - **tick off** (phrasal v.) = to mark items on a list as done/seen 逐一核对、打勾。
> - **a by-product** (n.) = an unintended secondary result 副产品、附带结果。
> - **practitioner** (n.) = someone who does an activity 从事者、爱好者。
> - **knowledgeable about** = 对……很了解、很在行。
> - **technical specifications** = detailed technical descriptions 技术规格、技术参数。
>
> **Main idea 主旨:** Trainspotting illustrates the educational point: chasing and ticking off engines turns hobbyists, *as a by-product*, into experts on railways. 火车观察记录印证了"教育"这一点:追寻并逐一记录机车,让爱好者"作为副产品"成为铁路专家。

**Paragraph 10 — Conclusion: passion and fulfilment**

Of course, all hobbies give pleasure, but the common factor in collecting is usually ==passion==: pleasure is putting [it] far too mildly. ## 🧠 Whole-passage summary 全文总结

**EN:** A psychologist explores **why millions of people collect**, laying out a series of motives: (1) **money** — collecting as *a means to an end*, plus the *triumph* of buying cheap and selling dear; (2) a **social life** with *like-minded people*; (3) the **thrill of the hunt** for something special, which gives an aimless life purpose (but success can leave you feeling empty); (4) **educational value** (stamps, fossils, and — as examples of expertise gained *as a by-product* — trainspotting and doll-making); (5) a psychological sense of **control** that helps with insecurity; and (6) the expression of **individualism**. The unifying thread is **passion**, and collecting can bring deep **personal fulfilment** — so, though it may look eccentric, it "has a lot going for it."

**中文:** 一位心理学家探讨**数百万人为何收藏**,并列出一系列动机:(1)**赚钱**——把收藏当作"手段",外加低买高卖的"胜利感";(2)与**志同道合者**的**社交生活**;(3)追寻特别之物的**寻猎快感**,它为漫无目的的人生赋予目标(但一旦成功反而可能空虚);(4)**教育价值**(邮票、化石,以及作为"副产品"习得专长的例子——火车观察记录与娃娃制作);(5)有助于应对不安全感的**掌控感**;(6)彰显**个性**。贯穿始终的核心是**激情**,收藏能带来深切的**个人成就感**——因此,尽管它看似古怪,却"大有可取之处"。
