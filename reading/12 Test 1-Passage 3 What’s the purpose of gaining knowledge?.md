**27**

A I would ==found an institution== where any person can find instruction in any subject.' That was the founder's ==motto== for Cornell University, and it seems ==an apt characterization== of the different university, also in the USA, where I currently teach philosophy. A student can prepare for a career in **resort management**, engineering, **interior design**, **accounting**, music, **law enforcement**, ==you name it==. But what would the founders of these two institutions have thought of a course called ==Arson for Profit==? **I kid you not**: we have it ==on the books==. Any ==undergraduates== who have ==met the academic requirements== can sign up for the course in our program in 'fire science'.

> [!quote] 🇨🇳 中文翻译 — Paragraph A
> "我要创办一所任何人都能学习任何学科的学院。"这是康奈尔大学创始人的箴言,而这句话似乎也很贴切地描述了我目前任教哲学的另一所大学(同样在美国)。学生可以为**度假村管理**、工程、**室内设计**、**会计**、音乐、**执法**等各种职业做准备——应有尽有。但这两所学院的创始人对一门名为"纵火牟利"的课程会作何感想呢?我可不是开玩笑:我们确实开设了这门课。任何满足了学术要求的本科生,都可以报名我们"火灾科学"专业中的这门课程。

> [!tip] 📝 Notes — Paragraph A
> **Vocabulary & phrases**
> - **found** (v.) = to establish / create an institution 创办、建立。⚠️ Not "find"! Note the pun 双关: *"**found** an institution where any person can **find** instruction."*(创办 vs. 找到)
> - **motto** (n.) = a short phrase expressing an ideal or guiding belief 箴言、座右铭。
> - **apt characterization** = a suitable / fitting description 贴切的描述。(*apt* = appropriate 恰当的)
> - **you name it** (idiom) = and anything else you can think of 诸如此类、应有尽有。
> - **on the books** (idiom) = officially exists / is officially recorded 正式列入(课程/记录)。
> - **undergraduate** (n.) = a student studying for a first (bachelor's) degree 本科生。
> - **met the academic requirements** = satisfied the necessary conditions to enrol 满足了学术(入学)要求。
> - **arson** (n.) = the crime of deliberately setting fire to property 纵火(罪)。
>
> **Main idea 主旨:** The author teaches philosophy at a US university that offers a huge range of **vocational courses** — including, surprisingly, one nicknamed *"Arson for Profit"* within its "fire science" program, open to any qualified undergraduate. 作者在一所开设大量职业课程的美国大学教哲学,其中竟包括"火灾科学"专业里一门名为"纵火牟利"的课,任何合格本科生都能报名。

> [!question] 🤔 Why is this course *surprising*? 为什么这门课令人意外?
> The surprise is built deliberately from three clashing elements 作者刻意用三重反差制造意外:
> 1. **Noble ideal vs. criminal-sounding course 崇高理想 vs. 像犯罪的课程.** The paragraph opens with a lofty motto — *"found an institution where any person can find instruction in any subject"* — painting the university as a place of pure, high-minded learning. Then comes a course called **"Arson for Profit"**. *Arson* is a **crime**, and *"for Profit"* makes it sound like the school teaches people **how to commit that crime for money**. 开篇的崇高箴言把大学塑造成纯粹求知之地,紧接着却是"纵火牟利"——纵火是犯罪,"牟利"更让它听起来像在教人犯罪赚钱。理想与课程之间的落差就是意外的核心。
> 2. **The ordinary list, then the odd one out 一串正常职业里的异类.** *resort management, engineering, interior design, accounting, music, law enforcement…* are all respectable careers; the reader relaxes — then **"Arson for Profit"** lands as the jarring exception. 前面全是体面职业,读者放松警惕,"纵火牟利"作为异类突然跳出,形成鲜明对比 (juxtaposition)。
> 3. **The author signals the shock on purpose 作者主动提示这令人震惊.** *"I kid you not"* (我可不是开玩笑) and *"we have it **on the books**"* (确实正式开设了) are things you only say about something hard to believe. 这两句话只会用在难以置信的事情上,等于告诉读者:是的,这听起来很疯狂,但它真实存在。
>
> **The trick 手法:** Paragraph A gives you **only the shocking name** and withholds the innocent explanation. Not until **Paragraph B** do we learn the course is really for arson *investigators* (纵火调查员), not arsonists. So the surprise is a deliberate **hook** — set up here, resolved later. 第一段只抛出惊人的课名,隐瞒无害的解释;直到第二段才揭示它其实是为纵火"调查员"而非纵火犯开设的。这个意外是刻意设下的**钩子**——此处埋设,后文解开。
> This ties into the whole passage: the **same knowledge (means/手段)** can sound criminal or noble depending on its **purpose (end/目的)** — the argument he builds in D–F. 这正呼应全文主旨:同样的知识(手段)因目的不同,可以显得像犯罪或高尚——即 D–F 段的论证。

C I realized this ==anew== when I was invited to speak before a class in marketing, which is another of our degree programs. The **regular instructor** is a colleague who appreciates the kind of ==ethical perspective== I can bring as a philosopher. There are endless ways I could have approached this assignment, but I ==took my cue from== the title of the course: 'Principles of Marketing'. It made me think to ask the students, =='Is marketing principled?'== After all, a subject matter can have principles in the sense of being ==codified==, having rules, as with football or chess, without being ==principled== in the sense of being ethical. Many of the students immediately assumed that the answer to my question about marketing principles was obvious: no. Just look at the ways in which ==everything under the sun== has been marketed; obviously it need not be done in a principled (=ethical) fashion.

> [!quote] 🇨🇳 中文翻译 — Paragraph C
> 当我受邀到市场营销课(这是我们的另一个学位专业)上讲课时,我重新意识到了这一点。这门课的常任讲师是一位同事,他很欣赏我作为哲学家所能带来的那种伦理视角。我本可以用无数种方式来处理这次讲课,但我从课程的名字——"市场营销原理(Principles of Marketing)"中获得了启发。这让我想到问学生们:"市场营销讲不讲原则(合不合道德)?"毕竟,一门学科可以在"被编纂成规则"这个意义上拥有原则(principles)——就像足球或象棋那样——却不一定在"合乎道德"这个意义上是有原则的(principled)。许多学生立刻认定,我这个关于营销原则的问题,答案显而易见:不讲。只要看看世间万物是如何被推销的就知道了;显然,营销并不一定要以合乎原则(即合乎道德)的方式进行。

> [!tip] 📝 Notes — Paragraph C
> **Vocabulary & phrases**
> - **anew** (adv.) = again, in a new way 重新、再次。
> - **ethical perspective** = a moral point of view 伦理/道德视角。(*ethical* = concerning right and wrong 与是非对错有关的)
> - **take one's cue from** (idiom) = to take guidance / inspiration from something 从……得到启示/提示。

> - ⚠️ **Key distinction 关键区分:** *having principles*(拥有规则,像象棋) ≠ *being principled*(合乎道德)。同一个词根,两种含义。
> - **everything under the sun** (idiom) = everything that exists 世间万物、一切事物。
> - **in a … fashion** = in a … way / manner 以……的方式。
>
> **Main idea 主旨:** Speaking to a marketing class, the author asks *"Is marketing principled?"* He separates two meanings of "principle": having **rules** (codified) vs. being **ethical** (principled). **30**

D Is that obvious? I made the suggestion, which may sound ==downright crazy== ==in light of== the evidence, that perhaps marketing is ==by definition== principled. My **inspiration** for this judgement is the philosopher ==Immanuel Kant==, who argued that **any body of knowledge** consists of ==an end (or purpose) and a means==.

> [!quote] 🇨🇳 中文翻译 — Paragraph D
> 这真的显而易见吗?我提出了一个观点——鉴于种种证据,它听起来可能相当疯狂:也许市场营销从定义上说就是讲原则(合乎道德)的。我这一判断的灵感来自哲学家伊曼努尔·康德,他认为任何知识体系都由一个"目的"(end)和一个"手段"(means)构成。

> [!tip] 📝 Notes — Paragraph D
> **Vocabulary & phrases**
> - **downright** (adv.) = completely, utterly 完全地、彻头彻尾地(强调:*downright crazy / rude* 简直疯了/十分无礼)。
> - **in light of** = considering / taking into account 鉴于、考虑到。
> - **by definition** = by its very nature; necessarily 根据定义、从本质上讲。
> - **a body of knowledge** = an organised field of information / study 一个知识体系/领域。
> - ⭐ **means vs. end** — Kant's key idea 康德的核心观点:
>   - **means** = the *how* 手段(方法/技术)。
>   - **end** = the *why* 目的(目标)。
>
> **Main idea 主旨:** The author proposes the opposite of the students' view — that marketing is **by definition principled**. His basis is Kant's claim that any field of knowledge has both a **means** and an **end**. 作者提出与学生相反的观点:营销**从定义上就是讲原则的**。其依据是康德——任何知识领域都同时包含**手段**与**目的**。

**31**

E Let us apply both the terms 'means' and 'end' to marketing. The students have signed up for a course in order to learn how to market effectively. But ==to what end==? There seem to be two main attitudes toward that question. One is that the answer is obvious: the purpose of marketing is to sell things and to make money. The other attitude is that the purpose of marketing is ==irrelevant==: Each person comes to the program and course with his or her own plans, and these need not even concern the ==acquisition of marketing expertise== as such. My proposal, which I believe would also be Kant's, is that neither of these attitudes ==captures the significance== of the end to the means for marketing. **==A field of knowledge or a professional endeavor i**s defined by both the means and the end; hence both deserve scrutiny.== Students need to study both how to achieve X, and also what X is.

> [!quote] 🇨🇳 中文翻译 — Paragraph E
> 让我们把"手段"和"目的"这两个术语都应用到市场营销上。学生们报名这门课,是为了学习如何有效地进行营销。但目的是什么呢?对这个问题似乎有两种主要态度。一种认为答案显而易见:营销的目的就是卖东西、赚钱。另一种态度认为营销的目的无关紧要:每个人都带着自己的计划来到这个专业和课程,而这些计划甚至不一定与获得营销专业知识本身有关。我的看法(我相信也会是康德的看法)是,这两种态度都没有抓住"目的"对于"手段"而言的重要意义。一个知识领域或一项专业事业,是由手段和目的共同定义的;因此两者都值得仔细审视。学生既需要学习如何实现X,也需要学习X究竟是什么。

> [!tip] 📝 Notes — Paragraph E
> **Vocabulary & phrases**
> - **to what end?** = for what purpose? 为了什么目的?
> - **irrelevant** (adj.) = not important / not connected to the matter 无关的、不相干的。
> - **acquisition** (n.) = the act of gaining or obtaining something 获得、习得(*acquisition of expertise* 专业知识的习得)。
> - **expertise** (n.) = expert skill or knowledge 专业知识、专长。
> - **capture the significance** = to express / grasp the importance of something 抓住/领会……的重要意义。
> - **endeavor** (n.) = a serious effort or attempt 事业、努力(*a professional endeavor* 一项专业事业)。
> - **hence** (adv.) = therefore, for this reason 因此、由此。
> - **scrutiny** (n.) = careful and detailed examination 仔细审查。→ *deserve scrutiny* 值得仔细审视。
>
> **⭐ Sentence to remember 值得背诵的句子:** *"A field of knowledge or a professional endeavor is defined by both the means and the end; hence both deserve scrutiny."* (一个知识领域或专业事业由手段与目的共同定义,因此两者都值得审视。)
>
> **Main idea 主旨:** Applying means/end to marketing: students learn *how* (means), but *to what end?* Two common views — (1) purpose is obvious (sell / make money); (2) purpose is irrelevant. **32**

F It is at this point that 'Arson for Profit' becomes ==supremely relevant==. That course is presumably all about means: how to detect and **prosecute** criminal activity. It is therefore assumed that the end is good in an ethical sense. When I ask fire science students to ==articulate== the end, or purpose, of their field, they eventually ==generalize to== something like, 'The ==safety and welfare== of society,' which seems right. As we have seen, someone could use the very same knowledge of means to achieve a much less ==noble end==, such as personal profit via **destructive, dangerous, ==reckless== activity.** But we would not call that firefighting. We have a separate word for it: arson. Similarly, if you employed the 'principles of marketing' in an unprincipled way, you would not be doing marketing. We have another term for it: ==fraud==. Kant gives the example of a doctor and a **poisoner**, who use the ==identical== knowledge to achieve their ==divergent ends==. We would say that one is **practicing medicine,** the other, murder.

> [!quote] 🇨🇳 中文翻译 — Paragraph F
> 正是在这一点上,"纵火牟利"这门课变得极为切题。这门课想必全都是关于手段的:如何侦查并**起诉**犯罪活动。因此人们假定,它的目的在伦理意义上是好的。当我要求火灾科学专业的学生清楚地表述他们这一领域的目的时,他们最终会概括为类似"社会的安全与福祉"这样的说法,这似乎是对的。正如我们所见,某人也可以运用完全相同的手段知识,去实现一个远不那么高尚的目的,比如通过**破坏性的、危险的、鲁莽的活动**来谋取个人私利。但我们不会把那称为消防。我们对此有一个单独的词:纵火。同样,如果你以不讲原则(不道德)的方式运用"市场营销原理",你所做的就不是营销了。我们对此还有另一个词:欺诈。康德举了医生和投毒者的例子:他们运用完全相同的知识,去实现各自截然不同的目的。我们会说,一个是在行医,另一个是在谋杀。

> [!tip] 📝 Notes — Paragraph F
> **Vocabulary & phrases**
> - **supremely relevant** = extremely relevant 极为切题、高度相关。(*supremely* = to the highest degree 极其)
> - **articulate** (v.) = to express (an idea) clearly in words 清楚地表达。
> - **generalize to** = to move from specifics to a broad statement 概括为、归纳为。
> - **welfare** (n.) = the health, happiness, and well-being of a group 福祉、福利。
> - **noble end** = a morally good / honourable purpose 高尚的目的。(*noble* = admirable 崇高的)
> - **reckless** (adj.) = careless about danger or consequences 鲁莽的、不计后果的。
> - **fraud** (n.) = the crime of deceiving people for financial gain 欺诈、诈骗。
> - **identical** (adj.) = exactly the same 完全相同的。
> - **divergent ends** = different / opposing purposes 截然不同的目的。(*divergent* = moving apart 分歧的)
>
> **⭐ Core analogy 核心类比 (same means → different ends 相同手段 → 不同目的):**
> | Same knowledge 相同知识 (means) | Good end 好的目的 | Bad end 坏的目的 |
> |---|---|---|
> | Fire science 火灾科学 | firefighting 消防 | **arson 纵火** |
> | Marketing principles 营销原理 | marketing 营销 | **fraud 欺诈** |
> | Medical knowledge 医学知识 | medicine (doctor) 行医 | **murder (poisoner) 谋杀** |
>
> **Main idea 主旨:** *"Arson for Profit"* teaches means and assumes a good end — *"safety and welfare of society."* But the same means used for a bad end isn't firefighting; it's **arson**. Likewise, unprincipled marketing is **fraud**, misused medicine is **murder**. So the **end** defines what the field truly is. "纵火牟利"教的是手段,并假定目的良善("社会的安全与福祉")。但同样的手段用于坏目的就不是消防而是**纵火**;不讲原则的营销是**欺诈**,滥用的医学知识是**谋杀**。因此,**目的**决定了一个领域的真正本质。

## 🧠 Whole-passage summary 全文总结

**EN:** The author (a philosophy professor) uses a **provocatively-named** course, *"Arson for Profit,"* to make a Kantian point: **every field of knowledge is defined by both a *means* (how) and an *end* (why).** The *same* skills can serve a good end or a bad one — firefighting vs. arson, medicine vs. murder, marketing vs. fraud. Therefore a discipline can't be judged by its techniques alone; its **purpose** is essential. His surprising conclusion: genuine marketing is, *by definition*, ethical ("principled") — because using its methods unethically turns it into something else entirely (fraud).

**中文:** 作者(一位哲学教授)借一门名字挑衅的课程——"纵火牟利"——阐明一个源自康德的观点:**任何知识领域都由"手段"(如何做)和"目的"(为何做)共同定义。** 相同的技能既可服务于善的目的,也可服务于恶的目的——消防 vs. 纵火、行医 vs. 谋杀、营销 vs. 欺诈。因此,评判一门学科不能只看其技术手段,其**目的**才是关键。他给出一个出人意料的结论:真正的市场营销**从定义上**就是合乎道德("讲原则")的——因为一旦以不道德的方式运用其方法,它就变成了完全不同的东西(欺诈)。
