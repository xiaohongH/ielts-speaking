**Paragraph 1 — A birthday gift that became a decade-long obsession**

In 2008, Peter Bellerby, who lived in London, wanted to give his father a ==model globe== for his ==eightieth birthday==. What ==seemed simple enough to start with== ==triggered== an almost ==obsessive==, ==decade-long journey==, ==marked by== ==a series of obstacles== that would have ==deterred== anyone ==less determined==. It ended with his establishing the world's only ==bespoke== globemaking company.

> 🔑 model globe 地球仪模型 · eightieth birthday 八十岁生日 · seemed simple enough to start with 起初看似很简单 · triggered 触发了 · obsessive 近乎执迷的 · decade-long journey 长达十年的历程 · marked by 以……为特征 · a series of obstacles 一系列障碍 · deterred 吓退/阻止 · less determined 意志没那么坚定的 · bespoke 定制的

> [!quote] 🇨🇳 中文翻译 — Paragraph 1
> 2008年,住在伦敦的彼得·贝尔利想送给父亲一个地球仪模型,作为他八十岁的生日礼物。起初看似再简单不过的一件事,却触发了一段近乎执迷、长达十年的历程,其间充满了一系列足以让意志稍弱之人望而却步的障碍。最终,他创立了世界上唯一一家定制地球仪的公司。

> - **a series of obstacles** = many difficulties one after another 一系列障碍。(*obstacle* = 障碍)

**Paragraph 2 — Not a quick online order — globemaking is ancient**

The first surprise in *The Globemakers*, Bellerby's ==account of== this ==impulsive enterprise==, is that obtaining such a globe was ==not simply a matter of== a ==quick online order== and a ==repressed sigh== at the shipping costs. After all, ==contrary to== ==stubbornly held popular views== of our ancestors' ==geographical ignorance==, we have known that the world is ==spherical== since at least the 6th century BCE. The ancient Greek philosopher Plato in his work *Phaedo* ==likened it to== a leather ball, while the ==accolade== of producing the ==first recorded globe== goes to the ancient Greek philosopher Crates of Mallus, who ==is said to have== made one in around 150 BCE. ==Surely, Bellerby reasoned, a good-quality globe wouldn't be difficult to find==.

> 🔑 account of 对……的记述 · impulsive enterprise 冲动之举/事业 · not simply a matter of 并非只是……那么简单 · quick online order 网上快速下单 · repressed sigh 强忍住的叹息 · contrary to 与……相反 · stubbornly held popular views 人们固执持有的流行看法 · geographical ignorance 对地理的无知 · spherical 球形的 · likened it to 把它比作 · accolade 荣誉 · first recorded globe 有记载的第一个地球仪 · is said to have 据说曾经 · wouldn't be difficult to find 不该难找

> [!quote] 🇨🇳 中文翻译 — Paragraph 2
> 《地球仪制造者》是贝尔利对这次冲动之举的记述。书中第一个令人意外之处在于:要得到这样一个地球仪,并不是在网上快速下单、再对运费暗自叹口气那么简单。毕竟,与人们固执持有的、认为我们的祖先对地理一无所知的流行看法相反,至少从公元前6世纪起,我们就已经知道世界是球形的。古希腊哲学家柏拉图在其著作《斐多篇》中,把地球比作一个皮球;而制作出有记载的第一个地球仪这一荣誉,则归于古希腊哲学家马卢斯的克拉特斯——据说他在约公元前150年制作了一个。贝尔利据此推断:一个优质的地球仪,按理说不该难找。

> [!tip] 📝 Notes — Paragraph 2
> **Vocabulary & phrases**
> - **account of** = a written description / narrative of 对……的记述、叙述。
> - **impulsive enterprise** = a venture begun on impulse, without much planning 一时冲动开始的事业。(*impulsive* = 冲动的;*enterprise* = 事业、进取之举)
> - **not simply a matter of** = not just as easy as 并非只是……那么简单。⭐ 好用句式。
> - **quick online order** = buying something fast on the internet 网上快速下单。= *internet purchase*。
> - **repressed sigh** = a sigh you hold back 强忍住的叹息。(*repress* = 压抑)
> - **contrary to** = in opposition to / unlike 与……相反。⭐ 作者用它来**否定**某种流行看法。
> - **stubbornly held popular views** = common beliefs people refuse to give up 人们固执持有的流行看法。
> - **geographical ignorance** = knowing little about geography 对地理的无知。
> - **spherical** (adj.) = shaped like a sphere / ball 球形的。
> - **likened it to** = compared it to 把它比作。(*liken A to B* = 把 A 比作 B)
> - **accolade** (n.) = praise / an honour 荣誉、赞誉。

>
> **⭐ Y/N/NG traps 判断题陷阱 (本段是重灾区):**
> 1. *contrary to stubbornly held popular views of our ancestors' geographical ignorance* → the writer **rejects** the idea that ancient people knew little about geography. 作者**否定**"古人地理知识贫乏"这一流行看法。
> 2. Plato *likened it to a leather ball* — that is **all**. No praise, no criticism. 柏拉图只是打了这个比方——**没有**说有没有人批评他。
> 3. Crates gets the *accolade* of the *first recorded globe* — **no** comment on whether it was accurate. 克拉特斯的荣誉是"有记载的第一个",**并未**评价是否精确。
> 4. *"Surely … wouldn't be difficult to find"* = Bellerby **did** assume a well-made globe would be easy to locate. 他**确实**以为优质地球仪不难找。
>
> **Main idea 主旨:** Buying a globe was not a quick online order. Humans have known the Earth is round since at least the 6th century BCE (Plato; Crates's first recorded globe c. 150 BCE), so Bellerby assumed a good one would be easy to find. 买地球仪并非网上随手一单。人类至少从公元前6世纪就知道地球是圆的(柏拉图;克拉特斯约公元前150年制作有记载的第一个地球仪),所以贝尔利以为优质地球仪不难找。

**Paragraph 3 — The craft had been largely forgotten**

Nearly ==two millennia later==, however, it seemed that the art of globemaking had been ==largely forgotten==. Even his trips to Morocco and India, where surely the ==knowledge of artisan cartographers== had been ==preserved==, ==drew a blank==.

**Paragraph 4 — Making his own: an oblate Earth, moulds, and disaster**

==Not one to be easily thwarted==, Bellerby decided to ==make his own== good-quality globe. In the process, ==almost everything that could possibly go wrong did so==. Even the shape of the Earth ==posed a problem==, as it is not quite a perfect sphere, but ==oblate== (slightly flattened at the poles). Having decided to ==compromise== and ==opt for== two half-spherical pieces that could be ==fitted together==, he was unable to discover anyone capable of ==casting moulds== with ==sufficient accuracy== to ensure that he would not be left with two half-spheres that were not quite the same ==circumference==. Even after he eventually ==resolved this issue==, ==extracting== these from the moulds resulted in piles of cracked ==plaster of Paris== and clouds of ==choking dust== in the workshop he had set up ==at the rear of his house==.

> [!quote] 🇨🇳 中文翻译 — Paragraph 4

**Paragraph 5 — Gores, glue, and a centimetre that swallows Chile**

This series of ==abortive experiments== taught Bellerby a lot about the challenges of making globes, which he ==communicates here to the reader==. Finding just the right way to ==prise== the globes from the mould — a **high-end** ==air compressor== finally ==did the trick== — and locating the right paper and inks with which to make the ==gores== (the sections of flat sheet mapping that are pasted onto the spherical globe) without the ink ==seeping out== to create a ==mushy, unreadable mess== took months and an ==alarming chunk out of his bank balance==. Bellerby's frustration at the ==painstaking process== of attaching the gores to the globe surface — after having found a glue with ==precisely the right adhesive qualities== — is ==palpable==. Right at the end of the process, he learnt that the paper had ==stretched slightly== and so the final one ==overlapped== the first by a centimetre (which may not seem a great deal, but when that represents 2 per cent of the Earth's diameter, it's equivalent to ==obliterating== the Himalayas or ==wiping out== Chile).

> 🔑 abortive experiments 失败的实验 · communicates here to the reader 在书中传达给读者 · prise 撬出 · air compressor 空气压缩机 · did the trick 奏效了 · gores 地球仪分瓣(戈尔) · seeping out 渗出 · mushy, unreadable mess 糊状、无法识读的乱摊子 · alarming chunk out of his bank balance 从存款里挖走惊人的一大块 · painstaking process 煞费苦心的过程 · precisely the right adhesive qualities 黏性恰到好处 · palpable 清晰可感的 · stretched slightly 略微拉伸 · overlapped 重叠 · obliterating 抹掉 · wiping out 抹去

> [!quote] 🇨🇳 中文翻译 — Paragraph 5
> 这一连串失败的实验,让贝尔利对制作地球仪的种种挑战了解颇深,他也在书中把这些传达给了读者。找到把地球仪从模具中撬出的正确方法——最终一台**高端**空气压缩机解决了这个问题——以及找到合适的纸张和油墨来制作"戈尔"(gores,即粘贴到球形地球仪表面的平面地图分瓣),同时不让油墨渗出、变成一团糊状、无法识读的乱摊子——这些花了数月时间,也从他的银行账户里挖走了惊人的一大块。在找到黏性恰到好处的胶水之后,把戈尔贴到地球仪表面这一煞费苦心的过程所带来的挫败感,在字里行间清晰可感。就在流程的最后关头,他才发现纸张略微拉伸了,于是最后一瓣与第一瓣重叠了整整一厘米(这或许看起来不算多,但当它相当于地球直径的2%时,就等于抹掉了喜马拉雅山脉,或抹去了整个智利)。

> [!tip] 📝 Notes — Paragraph 5
> **Vocabulary & phrases**
> - **abortive experiments** = attempts that failed before succeeding 失败的、无果的实验。(*abortive* = 失败的、夭折的)
> - **prise** (v., BrE; AmE *pry*) = to force sth open/out 撬开、撬出。
> - **did the trick** (idiom) = solved the problem; worked 奏效了、解决了问题。⭐
> - **gores** (n.) = tapered map panels pasted onto a globe 地球仪分瓣(把平面地图贴到球体上的那些瓣)。本段术语,考题常直接用这个词。
> - **seeping out** = leaking out slowly 慢慢渗出。(*seep* = 渗)
> - **mushy** (adj.) = soft and wet, like mush 糊状的、烂糊的。
> - **an alarming chunk out of his bank balance** = a worryingly large amount of his money 从存款里挖走惊人的一大块。(*chunk* = 一大块)
> - **painstaking** (adj.) = done with great care and a lot of time 煞费苦心的、一丝不苟的。⭐ **≠** working quickly。
> - **adhesive qualities** = how well glue sticks 黏性、粘合力。(*adhesive* = 黏合的/黏合剂)
> - **palpable** (adj.) = so strong it can almost be felt 清晰可感的、明显的。
> - **stretched slightly** = became a little longer/wider 略微拉伸。
> - **overlapped** (v.) = covered part of another 重叠、盖住一部分。
> - **obliterate / wipe out** = to destroy completely 彻底抹掉、消灭。
>
> **⭐ The unexpected issue 意外问题 (常考):** he had already found the right glue; the process was *painstaking* (slow, careful) — **then**, right at the end, the paper had stretched and the last gore overlapped the first by 1 cm. That is the surprise — not speed, not bad materials, not giving up. 胶水已经找对了,过程是**煞费苦心**(慢而仔细)的——**然后**在最后关头纸张拉伸、末瓣重叠1厘米。这才是意外——不是图快、不是材料差、也不是差点放弃。
>
> **Main idea 主旨:** Failed trials taught him the craft: an air compressor to free the globes; the right paper/ink for gores; painstaking gluing — and, unexpectedly at the end, stretched paper that overlapped by a centimetre (2% of Earth's diameter). 失败的试验教会他这门手艺:用空气压缩机脱模;用对的纸墨做戈尔;煞费苦心地粘贴——却在最后意外发现纸张拉伸、重叠一厘米(地球直径的2%)。

**Paragraph 6 — Kindred spirits: Behaim and Coronelli**

Bellerby's account of the technical challenges of globe production is ==interspersed with== a series of ==interludes== on great globemakers of the past and ==cartographic history== in general. ==Purists== might wish for more map-making details, but Bellerby clearly found a ==kindred spirit== in Martin Behaim. He was the Nuremberg entrepreneur who in 1492 created the *Erdapfel*, the world's ==oldest surviving globe==, beautifully finished by a workshop of painters and other craftsmen, only to find that the explorer Christopher Columbus had ==stumbled upon== the Americas the very same year, ==rendering his masterpiece instantly out of date==. Something of Bellerby's ==unflinching ambition== is ==reflected in== the even more heroic efforts of the Italian cartographer Vincenzo Coronelli, who, in the seventeenth century, created two globes for Louis XIV of France. It took him twenty years to complete the ==monstrous pair==, whose ==vast bulk== — each with a diameter of around four metres — can still be admired in the National Library of France in Paris.

> [!quote] 🇨🇳 中文翻译 — Paragraph 6

**Paragraph 7 — A lament for fading craft skills**

Although a ==celebration of the revival== of an ancient craft, Bellerby's book is also a ==lament== for the ==fading away== of ==centuries-old traditions==. When he ==embarked on== his globemaking ==odyssey==, he ==struggled to find== artisans with the skills to make the right moulds for the globes or ==foundries== that could shape the ==meridians== (the metal frames which ==girdle== globes) in **just** the right way. > - **foundries** (n.) = factories that cast metal 铸造厂。

## 🧠 Whole-passage summary 全文总结

**EN:** In 2008 Peter Bellerby set out to buy his father an eightieth-birthday globe. What **seemed simple** became a **decade-long**, almost **obsessive** quest through **obstacle** after obstacle, ending in the world's only **bespoke** globemaking company. A quality globe was **not** a **quick online order**: although humans have known the Earth is **spherical** since at least the 6th century BCE (Plato; Crates's first recorded globe), the craft had been **largely forgotten** — **shoddy** classroom models, unaffordable antiques, and blank trips to Morocco and India. Undeterred, he made his own. The Earth is **oblate**; moulds cracked; **gores** (map panels) needed the right paper, ink and glue. A **painstaking** pasting job ended with an unexpected 1 cm **overlap** from stretched paper. Historical **interludes** present Behaim as a **kindred spirit** and Coronelli as a mirror of **unflinching ambition**. The book **celebrates** the craft's **revival** but is also a **lament** for **fading** skills. The father got his gift **two years late**. The book is **hardly a blueprint for commercial success** — but it **stirs admiration** for past **craftsmanship** and for the successor who revived an almost **moribund** art.
