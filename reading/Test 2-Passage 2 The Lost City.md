**14**

**A** When the US explorer and ==academic== Hiram Bingham arrived in South America in 1911, he was ready for what was to be ==the greatest achievement of his life==: the exploration of the ==remote hinterland== to the west of Cusco, the old capital of the Inca empire in the Andes mountains of Peru. His goal was to ==locate the remains== of a city called Vitcos, the last capital of the Inca civilisation. Cusco lies on a ==high plateau== at an ==elevation== of more than 3,000 metres, and Bingham's plan was to ==descend== from this plateau along the valley of the Urubamba river, which takes a ==circuitous route== down to the Amazon and passes through an area of dramatic ==canyons== and mountain ranges.

> 🔑 academic 学者 · remote hinterland 偏远腹地 · locate the remains 找到遗迹 · high plateau 高原 · elevation 海拔 · descend 下降 · circuitous route 迂回路线 · canyons 峡谷

> [!quote] 🇨🇳 中文翻译 — Paragraph A

**B** When Bingham and his team ==set off== down the Urubamba in late July, they ==had an advantage over== ==travelers who had preceded them==: a track had recently been ==blasted== down the valley canyon to enable rubber to be brought up by ==mules== from the ==jungle==. Almost all previous travelers had left the river at Ollantaytambo and taken a high pass across the mountains to rejoin the river lower down, thereby ==cutting a substantial corner==, but also therefore never passing through the area around Machu Picchu.

**17**

**D** Bingham writes about the approach in ==vivid style== in his book. First, as he climbs up the hill, he describes the ==ever-present== possibility of deadly snakes, 'capable of making considerable springs when ==in pursuit of their prey=='; not that he sees any. Then there's ==a sense of mounting discovery== as he ==comes across== great sweeps of ==terraces==, then a ==mausoleum==, followed by ==monumental staircases== and, finally, the grand ==ceremonial buildings== of Machu Picchu. 'It seemed like an unbelievable dream … the sight held me ==spellbound== …' he wrote.

> 🔑 vivid style 生动的笔法 · ever-present 始终存在的 · in pursuit of their prey 追捕猎物 · a sense of mounting discovery 逐渐升腾的发现感 · comes across 偶然发现 · terraces 梯田 · mausoleum 陵墓 · monumental staircases 宏伟的阶梯 · ceremonial buildings 祭祀建筑 · spellbound 着迷的

> [!quote] 🇨🇳 中文翻译 — Paragraph D
> 宾厄姆在书中以生动的笔法描写了这次登临。首先,当他爬上山时,他描述了随时可能出现的致命毒蛇——"在追捕猎物时能进行相当远的跳跃"——尽管他其实一条也没见到。接着,随着他偶然发现大片的梯田、然后是一座陵墓、随后是宏伟的阶梯,最后是马丘比丘那些宏大的祭祀建筑,一种不断升腾的发现感油然而生。"这仿佛是一个难以置信的梦境……那景象令我如痴如醉……"他写道。

> [!tip] 📝 Notes — Paragraph D
> **Vocabulary & phrases**
> - **vivid style** = a lively, detailed way of writing 生动的笔法/文风。(*vivid* = 生动的、鲜明的)
> - **ever-present** (adj.) = always there 始终存在的、无处不在的。
> - **in pursuit of their prey** = chasing the animals they hunt 追捕猎物时。(*pursuit* = 追逐;*prey* = 猎物)
> - **a sense of mounting discovery** = a growing feeling of discovery 一种不断增强的发现感。(*mounting* = 逐渐增强的)
> - **come across** (phrasal v.) = to find by chance 偶然发现、无意中遇到。
> - **terraces** (n.) = flat platforms cut into a hillside for farming 梯田。
> - **mausoleum** (n.) = a large, grand tomb 陵墓。
> - **monumental staircases** = huge, impressive stairs 宏伟的阶梯。(*monumental* = 纪念碑般的、宏大的)

>
> **Main idea 主旨:** In vivid, dramatic prose, Bingham recounts the mounting thrill of discovering the terraces, tomb, staircases and ceremonial buildings — a sight that left him spellbound. 宾厄姆以生动戏剧化的文字,叙述了发现梯田、陵墓、阶梯与祭祀建筑时不断升腾的激动——那景象令他如痴如醉。

**E** We should remember, however, that *Lost City of the Incas* is a work of ==hindsight==, not written until 1948, many years after his journey. His ==journal entries== of the time reveal a much more ==gradual appreciation== of his achievement. He spent the afternoon at the ruins ==noting down the dimensions== of some of the buildings, then descended and rejoined his companions, to whom he seems to have said little about his discovery. At this stage, Bingham didn't realise ==the extent or the importance== of the site, nor did he realise what use he could make of the discovery.

> 🔑 hindsight 事后之明 · journal entries 日记 · gradual appreciation 逐渐的认识 · noting down the dimensions 记下尺寸 · the extent or the importance 规模或重要性

> [!quote] 🇨🇳 中文翻译 — Paragraph E
> 不过,我们应当记住,《印加失落之城》是一部凭事后之明写成的作品,直到1948年,即他那次旅程许多年之后才动笔。他当时的日记显示,他对自己这一成就的认识其实要渐进得多。那天下午他在遗迹处记下了一些建筑的尺寸,随后便下山与同伴会合,而他似乎并没有向他们多谈自己的发现。在这个阶段,宾厄姆既没有意识到这处遗址的规模或重要性,也没有意识到自己能如何利用这一发现。

> [!tip] 📝 Notes — Paragraph E
> **Vocabulary & phrases**
> - **hindsight** (n.) = understanding of an event only after it has happened 事后之明、事后认识。→ *a work of hindsight* 凭事后之明写成的作品。⭐ 高频词。
> - **journal entries** = individual records written in a diary 日记条目。
> - **gradual appreciation** = a slow, growing understanding 逐渐的认识/领会。(*appreciation* here = 认识、领会,not 欣赏)
> - **noting down the dimensions** = writing down the measurements 记下尺寸/大小。(*dimensions* = 尺寸、规模)
> - **the extent or the importance** = 规模(范围)或重要性。(*extent* = 程度、范围)
>
> **⭐ Key contrast 关键对比:** the 1948 **book** (dramatic, written with *hindsight*) vs. the 1911 **journal** (shows only *gradual* awareness). At the time, Bingham did **not** grasp the site's importance. 1948年的**书**(戏剧化、事后之明)vs. 1911年的**日记**(只显示逐渐的认识)。当时宾厄姆**并未**意识到遗址的重要性。⭐ IELTS 常考此处的"当时 vs. 事后"差别。
>
> **Main idea 主旨:** Bingham's dramatic book was written 37 years later; his actual 1911 journal shows he only *gradually* understood the find and, at the time, grasped neither its size nor importance. 宾厄姆那本戏剧化的书是37年后才写的;他1911年的真实日记表明,他对这一发现只是**逐渐**认识,当时既不知其规模也不知其重要性。

**F** However, soon after returning ==it occurred to him== that he could ==make a name for himself== from this discovery. When he came to write the National Geographic magazine article that ==broke the story to the world== in April 1913, he knew he had to produce a big idea. He wondered whether it could have been the ==birthplace== of the very first Inca, Manco the Great, and whether it could also have been what ==chroniclers== described as 'the last city of the Incas'. This term refers to Vilcabamba, the settlement where the Incas had ==fled from== Spanish ==invaders== in the 1530s. Bingham made ==desperate attempts== to prove this belief for nearly 40 years. Sadly, his vision of the site as both the beginning and end of the Inca civilisation, while a magnificent one, is ==inaccurate==. We now know that Vilcabamba actually lies 65 kilometres away in the depths of the jungle.

**G** One question that has ==perplexed== visitors, ==historians and archaeologists alike== ever since Bingham, is why the site seems to have been ==abandoned== before ==the Spanish Conquest==. There are no ==references to it== by any of the Spanish chroniclers – and if they had known of its existence so close to Cusco they would certainly have come ==in search of gold==. An idea which has ==gained wide acceptance== over the past few years is that Machu Picchu was a *moya*, a ==country estate== built by an Inca emperor to ==escape the cold winters== of Cusco, where ==the elite== could enjoy ==monumental architecture== and ==spectacular views==. Furthermore, the particular architecture of Machu Picchu suggests that it was constructed at the time of the greatest of all the Incas, the emperor Pachacuti (c. 1438-71). ==By custom==, Pachacuti's ==descendants== built other similar estates for their own use, and so Machu Picchu would have been abandoned after his death, some 50 years before the Spanish Conquest.

## 🧠 Whole-passage summary 全文总结

**EN:** In 1911, US explorer Hiram Bingham set out to find **Vitcos**, the last Inca capital, descending the Urubamba valley along a newly blasted track that — unlike earlier travelers' mountain shortcut — passed near **Machu Picchu**. On a dull morning, with little expectation, he climbed to the ruins. His 1948 book *Lost City of the Incas* describes the discovery in **vivid, spellbound** terms, but the passage stresses this is **hindsight**: his 1911 **journal** shows he only *gradually* grasped what he'd found and, at the time, understood neither its extent nor importance. Later, wanting to **make a name for himself**, he promoted a grand but **inaccurate** theory — that the site was both the Inca birthplace and their 'last city', Vilcabamba (actually 65 km away). The modern, widely accepted view is that Machu Picchu was a royal **country estate** built by the emperor **Pachacuti** and **abandoned** after his death, ~50 years before the Spanish Conquest.

**中文:** 1911年,美国探险家海勒姆·宾厄姆出发寻找印加最后的都城**维特科斯**,沿乌鲁班巴河谷、循一条新炸出的小道下行——与前人翻山抄近道不同,这条路正好经过**马丘比丘**附近。在一个阴沉的早晨,他不抱期待地爬上山去看遗迹。他1948年的著作《印加失落之城》以**生动、令人入迷**的笔法描写了这次发现,但本文强调这是**事后之明**:他1911年的**日记**显示,他对所发现之物只是**逐渐**认识,当时既不知其规模也不知其重要性。后来,出于**扬名**之心,他宣扬了一个宏大却**不准确**的理论——认为该址既是印加发源地又是其"最后之城"维尔卡班巴(其实在65公里外)。如今广为接受的看法是:马丘比丘是帕查库蒂皇帝所建的皇家**乡间庄园**,在他去世后(约西班牙征服前50年)被**遗弃**。
