## Intro — the four values and the thesis

In public discussion of business, we ==take certain values for granted==. Today I'm going to talk about four of them: collaboration, hard work, creativity and excellence. Most people would say they're all 'good things'. I'm going to suggest that's ==an over-simple view==. The trouble with these values is that they're ==theoretical concepts==, ==removed from the reality== of day-to-day business. ==Pursue values by all means==, but be prepared for what may happen as a result. They can actually ==cause damage==, which is not at all the intention. Business leaders generally try to ==do the right thing==. But all too often the right thing ==backfires==, if those leaders adopt values without understanding and managing the ==side effects== that arise. The values can easily ==get in the way of== what's actually intended.

> 🔑 take ... for granted 视为理所当然 · an over-simple view 过于简单的看法 · theoretical concepts 理论概念 · removed from the reality 脱离现实 · pursue ... by all means 尽管去追求 · cause damage 造成损害 · do the right thing 做正确的事 · backfires 事与愿违 · side effects 副作用 · get in the way of 妨碍

> [!quote] 🇨🇳 中文翻译 — Intro
> 在关于商业的公开讨论中,我们把某些价值观视为理所当然。今天我要谈其中的四个:协作、努力、创造力和卓越。大多数人会说它们都是"好东西"。我则要提出,这是一种过于简单的看法。这些价值观的问题在于,它们都是理论概念,脱离了日常商业的现实。尽管去追求价值观,但要对可能随之而来的后果有所准备。它们其实可能造成损害——而这绝非本意。商业领袖通常都想做正确的事。但如果这些领袖在采纳价值观时,不去理解并管理随之产生的副作用,那么正确的事往往会适得其反。这些价值观很容易妨碍到真正想要达成的目标。

> [!tip] 📝 Notes — Intro
> **Vocabulary & phrases**
> - **take ... for granted** = to assume sth is true/good without questioning it 视为理所当然。⭐ 高频短语。
> - **an over-simple view** = 过于简单化的看法。(*over-* 前缀 = 过度)
> - **theoretical concepts** = 理论(上的)概念。(*theoretical* = 理论的,与实践相对)
> - **removed from the reality** = disconnected from real life 脱离现实的。(*removed from* = 远离、脱离)
> - **pursue ... by all means** = 尽管去追求(*by all means* = 当然可以、尽管去做)。
> - **cause damage** = 造成损害。
> - **do the right thing** = 做正确的事。
> - **backfire** (v.) = to have the opposite effect to what was intended 事与愿违、适得其反。⭐ 全篇核心词。
> - **side effects** = unintended secondary consequences 副作用、附带的负面影响。
> - **get in the way of** = to obstruct / hinder 妨碍、阻碍。
>
> **Main idea 主旨:** The four "good" values (collaboration, hard work, creativity, excellence) are theoretical and can *backfire*: leaders who adopt them without managing the **side effects** may end up causing the very damage they meant to avoid. 四个"好"价值观其实是理论化的,可能**适得其反**:领袖若不管理其**副作用**,反而会造成他们本想避免的损害。

## Value 1 — Collaboration 协作

OK. So the first value I'm going to discuss is collaboration. Er, let me give you an example. On a management training course I once attended, we were put into groups and had to construct a bridge across a stream, using building blocks that we were given. The rule was that everyone in the team had to move at least one building block during the construction. This was intended to ==encourage teamwork==. But it was really a job best done by one person.

The other teams tried to collaborate on building the structure, and **==descended into confusion==, with everyone ==getting in each other's way==.** Our team leader ==solved the challenge brilliantly==. She simply asked everyone in the team to move a piece a few centimetres, to ==comply with the rule==, and then let the person in the team with ==an aptitude for== puzzles like this build it alone. We finished before any other team. My point is that the task wasn't really ==suited to== teamworking, so why make it one?

Teamwork can also lead to ==inconsistency== - a common cause of poor sales. In the case of a smartphone that a certain company launched, one director wanted to ==target the business market==, and another demanded it was aimed at consumers. The company wanted both directors to be involved, so gave the product a ==consumer-friendly== name, but marketed it to companies. The result was that it ==met the needs of neither group==. It would have been better to let one director or the other ==have his way==, not both.

> 🔑 encourage teamwork 鼓励团队合作 · descended into confusion 陷入混乱 · getting in each other's way 相互妨碍 · solved the challenge brilliantly 出色地解决了难题 · comply with the rule 遵守规则 · an aptitude for 对……的天赋 · suited to 适合 · inconsistency 不一致 · target the business market 面向商业市场 · consumer-friendly 迎合消费者的 · met the needs of neither group 两边需求都没满足 · have his way 按自己的意思来

> [!quote] 🇨🇳 中文翻译 — Collaboration
> 好,我要讨论的第一个价值观是协作。呃,我举个例子。在我曾参加的一次管理培训课上,我们被分成小组,要用发给我们的积木在小溪上搭一座桥。规则是:搭建过程中,队里每个人都必须至少移动一块积木。这本意是为了鼓励团队合作。但这其实是一件最好由一个人来做的活儿。
> 
> 其他小组都试图协作搭建,**结果陷入混乱,人人都在互相妨碍**。而我们的队长出色地解决了这个难题。她只是让队里每个人把一块积木挪动几厘米,以遵守规则,然后让队里一位对这类拼搭有天赋的人独自去搭。我们比其他任何小组都先完成。我想说的是:这个任务本来就不适合团队协作,==那又何必硬把它变成协作任务呢?==
> 
> 团队合作还会导致不一致——这是销售不佳的常见原因。以某公司推出的一款智能手机为例,一位总监想瞄准商业市场,另一位则坚持要面向普通消费者。公司希望两位总监都参与,于是给产品起了一个迎合消费者的名字,却又把它推销给企业。结果是,它两边的需求都没能满足。当初还不如让其中一位总监说了算,而不是两位都要照顾。

> **Vocabulary & phrases**

> - **descended into confusion** = fell into chaos 陷入混乱。(*descend into* = 沦为、陷入)
> - **getting in each other's way** = 相互妨碍、碍事。
> - **solved the challenge brilliantly** = 出色地解决了难题。(*brilliantly* = 出色地)
> - **comply with the rule** = to obey the rule 遵守规则。(*comply with* = 遵守)
> - **an aptitude for** = a natural ability for 对……的天赋/才能。

> - **inconsistency** (n.) = lack of consistency; things not matching 不一致、前后矛盾。
> - **target the business market** = 以商业市场为目标。
> - **consumer-friendly** (adj.) = appealing to ordinary consumers 迎合/方便消费者的。

>
> **⭐ The catch 陷阱:** collaboration is not always good. (1) Forcing teamwork onto a **solo task** → confusion (bridge). (2) Making everyone collaborate → a **compromise that pleases nobody** (smartphone). 协作并非总是好事:(1)把本该一个人做的任务硬凑成团队 → 混乱(搭桥);(2)让所有人都参与 → 谁都不满意的折中(手机)。
>
> **Main idea 主旨:** Two examples show collaboration backfiring: a bridge task best done alone, and a smartphone that pleased neither market because two directors were both accommodated. 两个例子说明协作会适得其反:搭桥本该独自完成;手机因迁就两位总监而两个市场都没讨好。

## Value 2 — Hard work (Industriousness) 勤奋

Now ==industriousness==, or hard work. It's easy to ==mock== people who say they work hard: after all, a ==**hamster**== running around in a wheel is working hard - and ==getting nowhere==. 

Of course hard work is valuable, but only when ==properly targeted==. Otherwise it ==wastes the resources== that companies value most - time and energy. And that's bad for the organisation. 

There's a management model that groups people according to four criteria: clever, hard-working, stupid and lazy. 

Here 'lazy' means having ==a rational determination not to carry out unnecessary tasks==. It doesn't mean trying to avoid work altogether. 

\\\Most people display two of these characteristics, and the most valuable people are those who are both ==clever and lazy==: they possess ==intellectual clarity==, and they ==don't rush into making decisions==. They come up with solutions to save the time and energy spent by the stupid and hard-working group. Instead of ==throwing more man-hours at a problem==, the clever and lazy group looks for ==a more effective solution==.

> 🔑 industriousness 勤奋 · mock 嘲笑 · getting nowhere 毫无进展 · properly targeted 精准定位的 · wastes the resources 浪费资源 · a rational determination not to carry out unnecessary tasks 理性地决定不做无谓的事 · clever and lazy 又聪明又"懒" · intellectual clarity 思路清晰 · don't rush into making decisions 不草率做决定 · throwing more man-hours at a problem 靠堆工时解决问题 · a more effective solution 更有效的解决办法

> [!quote] 🇨🇳 中文翻译 — Hard work
> 接下来是勤奋,也就是努力工作。嘲笑那些声称自己很努力的人是很容易的:毕竟,一只在转轮里不停奔跑的==仓鼠==也很"努力"——却哪儿也去不了。努力当然有价值,但前提是方向精准。否则它就会浪费企业最看重的资源——时间和精力。而这对组织是有害的。有一个管理模型按四个标准给人分类:聪明、勤奋、愚笨、懒惰。这里的"懒"指的是理性地决意不去做那些不必要的任务,而不是想彻底逃避工作。大多数人会表现出其中两种特征,而最有价值的人是那些既聪明又"懒"的人:他们思路清晰,并且不会草率地做决定。他们能想出解决办法,省下"愚笨又勤奋"那类人所耗费的时间和精力。与其向一个问题投入更多工时,聪明又"懒"的人会去寻找一个更有效的解决方案。

> [!tip] 📝 Notes — Hard work
> **Vocabulary & phrases**
> - **industriousness** (n.) = the quality of being hard-working 勤奋。(*industrious* adj. = 勤勉的;≠ *industrial* 工业的)
> - **mock** (v.) = to laugh at / ridicule 嘲笑、取笑。
> - **getting nowhere** = making no progress 毫无进展、原地踏步。
> - **properly targeted** = aimed in the right direction 方向精准的、有的放矢的。
> - **wastes the resources** = 浪费资源(here: time and energy 时间与精力)。
> - **a rational determination not to carry out unnecessary tasks** = 理性地决心不去做不必要的任务。(*rational* = 理性的;*carry out* = 执行)
> - **clever and lazy** = 又聪明又"懒"(此处"懒"是褒义:不做无用功)。
> - **intellectual clarity** = clear thinking 思路清晰、头脑清楚。
> - **don't rush into making decisions** = 不草率地做决定。(*rush into* = 仓促做)
> - **throwing more man-hours at a problem** = solving sth just by adding more work hours 靠堆更多工时来解决问题。(*man-hours* = 工时)
> - **a more effective solution** = 更有效的解决方案。
>
> **⭐ The catch 陷阱:** hard work only helps when **properly targeted**; untargeted effort is like a hamster wheel — busy but *getting nowhere*. The ideal worker is **clever + lazy**: finds efficient solutions instead of adding man-hours. 努力只有在**方向精准**时才有用;无方向的努力就像仓鼠转轮——很忙却**毫无进展**。理想员工是**聪明+"懒"**:找高效方案,而非堆工时。
>
> **Main idea 主旨:** Hard work is valuable only when targeted; otherwise it wastes time and energy. The most valuable people are "clever and lazy" — they find efficient solutions rather than piling on man-hours. 努力只有在有方向时才有价值,否则浪费时间精力。最有价值的人"聪明又懒"——找高效办法而非硬堆工时。

## Value 3 — Creativity 创造力

Next we come to creativity. This often works well - creating an ==attention-grabbing== TV commercial, for example, might lead to increased sales. But it isn't always a good thing. Some ==advertising campaigns== are remembered for their creativity, without having any effect on sales. 
This happened a few years ago with the launch of a chocolate bar: ==subsequent research== showed that plenty of consumers ==remembered the adverts, but had no idea what was being advertised==.
The trouble is that the creator ==derives pleasure from== coming up with the idea, and ==wrongly assumes== the audience for the campaign will share that feeling. 
A company that ==brings out== thousands of new products may seem more creative than a company that only has a few, but it may be ==too creative==, and make smaller profits. 
Creativity ==needs to be targeted==, to solve a problem that the company has identified. Just coming up with more and more ==novel products== isn't necessarily a good thing.

> 🔑 attention-grabbing 吸引眼球的 · advertising campaigns 广告活动 · subsequent research 后续调查 · remembered the adverts, but had no idea what was being advertised 记得广告,却不知在推销什么 · derives pleasure from 从……中获得乐趣 · wrongly assumes 错误地以为 · brings out 推出 · too creative 过度创新 · needs to be targeted 需有针对性 · novel products 新奇产品

> [!quote] 🇨🇳 中文翻译 — Creativity
> 接下来我们谈创造力。它常常很奏效——比如,制作一支吸引眼球的电视广告,可能带来销量的增长。但它并不总是好事。有些广告活动因其创意而被人记住,却对销量毫无影响。几年前一款巧克力棒上市时就发生了这种情况:后续调查显示,许多消费者记得那些广告,却根本不知道广告在推销什么。问题在于,创作者从想出创意本身获得了乐趣,并错误地以为广告的受众也会有同样的感受。一家推出成千上万种新产品的公司,也许看上去比只有寥寥几款产品的公司更有创造力,但它可能"创新过度",反而利润更低。创造力需要有针对性,用来解决公司已经识别出的某个问题。一味地推出越来越多新奇的产品,未必是好事。

> [!tip] 📝 Notes — Creativity
> **Vocabulary & phrases**
> - **attention-grabbing** (adj.) = designed to catch attention 吸引眼球的、抓人眼球的。
> - **advertising campaigns** = organised sets of adverts 广告活动/宣传战役。
> - **subsequent research** = research done afterwards 后续的研究/调查。(*subsequent* = 随后的)
> - **remembered the adverts, but had no idea what was being advertised** = 记得广告,却不知道广告的是什么(创意抢镜、产品被忽略)。
> - **derive pleasure from** = to get enjoyment from 从……中获得乐趣。
> - **wrongly assumes** = incorrectly believes 错误地假定/以为。
> - **bring out** (phrasal v.) = to launch / release a product 推出、发布(产品)。
> - **too creative** = 过度创新(以致偏离目的)。
> - **needs to be targeted** = must be aimed at a specific goal 需要有针对性。
> - **novel products** = new and unusual products 新奇的产品。(*novel* adj. = 新颖的)
>
> **⭐ The catch 陷阱:** creativity can be memorable yet **useless** — the chocolate advert people recalled without knowing the product. Creators wrongly assume the audience shares their delight. Being *too creative* (too many products) can *lower* profits. 创意可能令人难忘却**无用**——巧克力广告被记住却没人知道产品。创作者错以为观众和自己一样兴奋。"过度创新"(产品太多)反而**降低**利润。
>
> **Main idea 主旨:** Creativity often boosts sales but can backfire: memorable-but-ineffective ads (the chocolate bar) and companies that are "too creative" and make less profit. Creativity must be targeted at a real problem. 创造力常促销,但会适得其反:难忘却无效的广告(巧克力棒),以及"过度创新"、利润更低的公司。创造力必须针对真实问题。

## Value 4 — Excellence 卓越

And finally, excellence. We all know companies that claim they '==strive for excellence=='', but it takes a long time to achieve excellence. In business, ==being first with a product is more profitable than having the best product==. 
A major study of company performance compared ==pioneers== - that is, companies bringing out the first version of a particular product – with ==followers==, the companies that copied and improved on that product. 
The study found that the pioneers ==commanded an average market share of== 29 percent, while the followers achieved less than half that, only 13 percent - even though their product might have been better. 
Insisting on excellence in everything we do is ==time-consuming==, wastes energy and leads to ==losing out on opportunities==. 
Sometimes, ==second-rate work== is more worthwhile than excellence.
'Make sure it's excellent' sounds like a good approach to business, but the =='just-get-started' approach== is likely to be more successful.

> 🔑 strive for excellence 追求卓越 · being first with a product is more profitable than having the best product 抢先推出比拥有最好产品更赚钱 · pioneers 先行者 · followers 跟随者 · commanded an average market share of 占据平均市场份额 · time-consuming 耗时的 · losing out on opportunities 错失机会 · second-rate work 二流的工作 · 'just-get-started' approach "先干起来"的做法

> [!quote] 🇨🇳 中文翻译 — Excellence
> 最后是卓越。我们都知道有些公司声称自己"追求卓越",但要达到卓越需要很长时间。在商业中,抢先推出一款产品,比拥有最好的产品更赚钱。一项关于公司业绩的重要研究,把"先行者"——即率先推出某种产品第一个版本的公司——与"跟随者"——即模仿并改进该产品的公司——进行了比较。研究发现,先行者占据了平均29%的市场份额,而跟随者所获得的还不到其一半,仅有13%——即便他们的产品可能更好。在所有事情上都坚持卓越,是费时的、浪费精力的,还会导致错失良机。有时候,二流的活儿反而比卓越更有价值。"务必做到卓越"听起来像是个不错的经商之道,但"先干起来"的做法很可能更成功。

> [!tip] 📝 Notes — Excellence
> **Vocabulary & phrases**
> - **strive for excellence** = to try hard to achieve the highest quality 追求卓越。(*strive for* = 力求)
> - **being first with a product is more profitable than having the best product** = 抢先推出产品,比拥有最好的产品更赚钱。⭐ 本段核心句。
> - **pioneers** (n.) = companies that launch a product first 先行者、开拓者。
> - **followers** (n.) = companies that copy and improve it later 跟随者、模仿者。
> - **commanded an average market share of** = held/controlled an average market share of 占据了……的平均市场份额。(*command* here = 占据、掌握)
> - **time-consuming** (adj.) = taking a lot of time 耗时的。
> - **losing out on opportunities** = missing chances 错失机会。(*lose out on* = 错过)
> - **second-rate work** = work of lower quality 二流的、次等的工作。
> - **'just-get-started' approach** = doing it quickly rather than perfectly "先干起来/先开始再说"的做法。
>
> **⭐ The catch 陷阱:** chasing excellence is slow. Data: **pioneers 29% vs. followers 13%** market share — being *first* beats being *best*. So "just get started" often wins over "make it excellent". 追求卓越很慢。数据:**先行者29% vs. 跟随者13%**——**抢先**胜过**做到最好**。所以"先干起来"常胜过"务求卓越"。
>
> **Main idea 主旨:** Excellence takes too long; a study shows first-movers (pioneers, 29%) beat better-but-later followers (13%). "Just get started" usually beats "make it excellent." 卓越太耗时;研究显示抢先者(先行者,29%)胜过更好但更晚的跟随者(13%)。"先干起来"通常胜过"务求卓越"。

## 🧠 Whole-passage summary 全文总结

**EN:** The speaker challenges the assumption that four business values — **collaboration, hard work, creativity, excellence** — are automatically "good things." Each is only useful when managed, and each has a **side effect that can backfire**: (1) *collaboration* forced onto solo tasks causes confusion, and pleasing everyone pleases no one (the smartphone); (2) *hard work* wastes time and energy unless **properly targeted** — the ideal worker is "clever and lazy"; (3) *creativity* can produce memorable but **ineffective** campaigns (the chocolate bar), and being *too* creative lowers profits; (4) *excellence* takes too long — a study shows **pioneers (29% market share) beat better-but-later followers (13%)**, so "just get started" usually wins. The lesson: pursue values, but understand and manage their side effects, and keep them **targeted** at a real goal.

**中文:** 讲者挑战了一个假设——协作、努力、创造力、卓越这四个商业价值观天然就是"好东西"。每一个只有在被妥善管理时才有用,而每一个都有可能**适得其反的副作用**:(1)把**协作**强加于本该独立完成的任务会造成混乱,想讨好所有人结果谁都讨好不了(智能手机);(2)**努力**若无精准方向,只会浪费时间和精力——理想员工是"又聪明又懒";(3)**创造力**可能造出令人难忘却**无效**的广告(巧克力棒),而"过度创新"会降低利润;(4)**卓越**太耗时——研究显示**先行者(29%市场份额)胜过更好但更晚的跟随者(13%)**,所以"先干起来"通常更成功。结论是:可以追求价值观,但要理解并管理其副作用,并让它们始终**对准真实目标**。
