`base sth on` means to use something as the foundation or reason for something else.

Pattern:
- base A on B = A is built from B

Examples:
- We based our design on user feedback.
- The movie is based on a true story.
- They based the project on the theme of birds.

Related forms:
- be based on (passive): The project is based on bird behavior.
- basis (noun): User feedback is the basis of our design.

Common mistake:
- Incorrect: base on something
- Correct: base the project on something / be based on something

## Direction Language Notes (IELTS Listening)

### 1) “a corridor running left from here”
- Here, **run** does not mean moving fast.
- For roads/corridors/paths, **run** means **extend in a direction**.
- So this means: a corridor that extends to the left from this point.

Why **left** (not always “to the left”)?
- **running left from here**: direction-focused, concise, common in speech.
- **running to the left from here**: also correct, slightly more explicit.
- **to the left of X** is usually used for relative position (not route direction).

### 2) “go along that”
- **go along + path/corridor/road** = move forward following that route.
- **that** refers to the corridor mentioned before.
- Full form: “go along that corridor.”

Difference:
- **go to** = destination-focused
- **go along** = route/path-focused

### 3) “the door facing you at the end”
- Meaning: when you reach the end, the door directly in front of you is the correct one.

Natural version:
- “There is a corridor running left from here. If you go along it, the door facing you at the end is the entrance to the cafe.”
