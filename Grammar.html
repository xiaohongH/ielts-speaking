1 Past Tense When Telling Stories

The Rule — use past tense consistently when the story is over
📌 Core Rule
When you tell a story about something that already happened, stay in the past tense throughout. Switching to present tense mid-story sounds unnatural and confuses the listener.

The most common tenses in storytelling:
Present Perfect
I have been leading…
→
Simple Past
Yesterday was…
→
Past Continuous
I was preparing…
→
Future-in-Past
…so we could test it
❌ What you said
"The engineers had to finish the coding work and demo it to me… so we can test it before pushing it live."
✅ Band 8 fix
"…so we could test it before pushing it live."
Because the testing is in the past — "could" is the past form of "can".
❌ What you said
"…the problem is verse that I got an urgent task…"
✅ Band 8 fix
"…the problem was that I got an urgent task…"
  • I thought I could handle everything, but then my boss gave me an urgent task. ✓ all past
  • We prepared everything so that we could test before launch. ✓ could = past of can
  • She was nervous, so she passed her anxiety down to the team. ✓ was → passed
🧠 Quick Quiz
Choose the correct form: "We finished the demo so that we ___ test the product."
✅ "Could" — past form of "can", used when the main story is in the past.
Choose the correct form: "The problem ___ that my boss gave me extra work."
✅ "Was" — the whole situation happened yesterday, so stay in past tense.

2 Adjective vs. Adverb — "To be honest" not "honestly"

Fixed Phrases Using Adjectives — common in spoken English
📌 Core Rule
Some common speaking phrases use an adjective (not an adverb), because they describe the speaker, not the verb:

To be + adjective → "To be honest…", "To be fair…", "To be frank…"

Think of it as short for: "If I am being honest with you, I should say that…"
❌ What you said
"To be honestly, I'm fed up with this management style."
✅ Band 8 fix
"To be honest, I'm fed up with this management style."
❌ Avoid✅ Use insteadMeaning
To be honestlyTo be honestSignalling you're about to say something direct
To be franklyTo be frank / FranklySaying something bluntly
To be fairnessTo be fairGiving credit to the other side
Honestly speakingHonestly / To be honestBoth work; "Honestly" alone is fine
🧠 Quick Quiz
Which is correct? "___, I'm a bit frustrated with how things are managed."
✅ "To be honest" — it's a fixed adjective phrase, not an adverb.

3 Choosing the Right Verb Phrase

Natural Verb Collocations — the words that "go together" in English
📌 Core Rule
In English, certain verbs naturally pair with certain nouns or situations. Using the wrong verb sounds awkward even if the meaning is clear. This is called collocation — learning which words go together is key to sounding natural.
❌ What you said✅ Natural versionWhy
didn't know how to do with usdidn't know how to handle it"Handle" = deal with a difficult situation. "Do with" is not a natural phrase here.

concentrate on the demoing thinggive the demo my full attention / stay focused on the demo"Concentrate on" is correct but "stay focused on" sounds more fluent and natural.
more high-level than memore senior than me"Senior" is the standard professional word for higher rank. "High-level" usually describes tasks, not people.
out of electricityrunning on empty / completely drainedNative idioms for exhaustion. "Running on empty" is very vivid and natural.

"If we can't get their ___, the project might be cancelled."
✅ "Buy-in" — the natural business phrase for getting decision-makers on board.
"By the end of the day, I was completely ___."
✅ "Drained" or "running on empty" — natural idioms for exhaustion.

Adding, Contrasting & Sequencing — the glue of fluent speech
📌 Core Rule
Fluent English doesn't just list ideas — it connects them. Using the right connector signals the relationship between your ideas (adding, contrasting, consequence, sequence), which is exactly what IELTS examiners look for under Coherence & Cohesion.

5 Small but Important — Articles & Prepositions

The Little Words That Matter — easy to fix, big impact on fluency
📌 Core Rule
Articles (a / an / the) and prepositions (of / on / at / with) are the most common mistakes for non-native speakers — but also the easiest to fix once you know the patterns. Native speakers notice these instantly.
❌ What you said✅ FixRule
on top off thaton top of thatFixed phrase: always "on top OF"
had her planhad a planIt's your own plan — use the indefinite article "a"
a presentation to the big managera presentation to senior management"Senior management" is the set phrase — no article needed
responsible for heyresponsible for the overall…Specific role → use "the"
improve my English and to getimprove my English and getParallel structure — don't add "to" again after "and" in an infinitive list
💡 Parallel Structure Rule
When you connect two verbs with "and", don't repeat "to":

❌ "I need to improve my English and to get a Band 8."
✅ "I need to improve my English and get a Band 8."

❌ "She had to finish the code and to demo it."
✅ "She had to finish the code and demo it."
🧠 Quick Quiz
"I want to improve my English and ___ a Band 8 on the IELTS."
✅ "get" — parallel structure: "to improve… and get…". Don't repeat "to" after "and".
"She was the one responsible ___ the overall project."
✅ "responsible for" — this is a fixed collocation. Always "responsible FOR something".

6 Bonus — Expressing Emotions Precisely

Going Beyond "Tired" and "Sad" — emotional vocabulary for Band 8+
📌 Why this matters
IELTS Band 8 rewards precise lexical choice. Instead of simple words like "tired", "sad", or "stressed", use specific emotional vocabulary that shows exactly what you felt — and why.

🌟 Your sentence — upgraded
❌ "I felt very tired and sad."

✅ "The things she said left me feeling heartbroken and completely drained."
Note the structure: "left me feeling + adjective" — this is a very natural and powerful construction.
🧠 Quick Quiz
Which best describes: you've been dealing with the same problem for months and can't take it anymore?
✅ "Fed up" = patience worn out after a long time. "Burned out" = emotional and physical exhaustion from sustained effort. Both are precise and natural.
