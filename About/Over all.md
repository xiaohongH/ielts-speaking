> [!NOTE]
> **“data governance”** is not a generic policy term — it means the mechanisms that define and enforce **who can see what data, under what conditions**.
> 
> For your JD example, it likely includes:
> 
> - **Data classification**: tagging data by sensitivity level (public/internal/restricted).
> - **Access control model**: role- and scenario-based permissions (BU, internal ops, merchant, brand partner).
> - **Exposure rules**: what fields/metrics can be shown externally vs. only internally.
> - **Approval workflows**: who can request, approve, and audit access changes.
> - **Auditability**: logs for who accessed/shared what data and when.
> - **Policy enforcement in products/APIs**: rules applied automatically so restricted data cannot be leaked through dashboards, exports, or interfaces.
> 
> A more precise phrase you can use in interviews:
> 
> - **“I built data access governance and permission controls.”**
> 
> Or one sentence version:
> 
> - **“By data governance, I mean classification, permission, and enforcement mechanisms that control cross-party data visibility and prevent inappropriate external exposure.”**
