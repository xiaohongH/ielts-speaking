
Part 1 focuses on **fluency**. Answer quickly, keep it short and simple: give a direct answer, then one quick reason or example (2–3 sentences). Use contractions and natural fillers, and don't memorise word-for-word.

---

## 1. Work / Study *(almost always asked first)*

**Do you work or are you a student?**
> I'm working at the moment. I've got a job in e-commerce, and I've been doing it for about five years now.

**Do you like your job?**
> Yeah, I do actually. The pay's really good and I feel like I'm doing something meaningful — the only downside is that the workload's always pretty heavy.

**Why did you choose this job?**
> To be honest, I've wanted to be a product manager since university. I love the idea of creating something that could actually change the world.

**Would you like to change your job in the future?**
> Maybe one day. For now I'm happy where I am, but eventually I'd like something with a better work-life balance.

**What skills do you need for your job?**
> Communication and problem-solving, mainly. In e-commerce things move really fast, so you have to stay flexible and work well with all kinds of teams.

**You said you enjoy your job, but you also mentioned the workload is quite heavy. Isn't that a bit contradictory?**
> Ha, good point! But not really. I don't mind being busy — it gives me a sense of achievement. It's just about finding the right balance.

## 2. Hometown

**Where are you from?**
> I'm from a small town in the south of China. It's got beautiful scenery — lots of mountains and rivers — though it used to be quite a poor area.

**Do you like your hometown?**
> Not really, to be honest. I love the fresh air, but in such a small town everyone knows everyone, which feels a bit suffocating. So going back always leaves me feeling drained.

**Is it a good place for young people?**
> Not really, to be honest. There aren't many job opportunities, so a lot of young people move to bigger cities.

**How has your hometown changed in recent years?**
> Quite a lot, actually. The roads and buildings are much better now, and people are generally far better off than they used to be.

**Would you ever move back there?**
> Probably not. It's a lovely place to visit, but I've got used to the freedom and the opportunities you only really get in a big city.

## 3. Home / Where you live

**Do you live in a house or an apartment?**
> I live in an apartment I rent near my company — well, I just rent one of the rooms. It's small, but it's got everything I need.

**What do you like about where you live?**
> Mainly the location and the price. The rent's pretty affordable, and it's close to work — I can get there in about 30 minutes, which isn't bad at all.

**How long have you lived there?**
> Only about a year. Actually, I move almost every year — once the lease is up, the agency always puts the rent up, so I usually just find somewhere new instead.

**Would you like to move somewhere bigger in the future?**
> Definitely. It's fine for now, but one day I'd love to move into a proper place of my own, where I don't have to share with anyone.

**Do you like cooking?**

**Do you prefer eating at home or eating out?**

## 9. Reading

**Do you enjoy reading?**
> I really do. I don't always get much time for it, but I try to read most nights before bed — it's a great way to unwind and switch off.

**What kind of books do you like?**
> All sorts of things, but mainly historical novels and philosophy books. I love diving deep into big ideas — the nature of the world, human nature, and the search for truth.

**Do you prefer paper books or e-books?**
> Paper books, definitely. I stare at screens all day for work, so there's something relaxing about holding a real book and turning the pages.

**Did you read a lot as a child?**
> Yeah, quite a bit. I mostly read fairy tales and comics back then — nothing too serious, but they really sparked my imagination.

**How often do you read?**
> Pretty much every day, even if it's just for twenty minutes before bed. It's become a habit I'd really miss if I skipped it.

**Do you think reading is important for children?**
> Absolutely. It builds their vocabulary and imagination from an early age, and I think it helps them become more thoughtful, curious people.

**Book types — useful vocabulary**
- **fiction** — 小说类 (made-up stories); opposite is **non-fiction** 纪实类
- **novel** — 长篇小说
- **historical novel** — 历史小说
- **philosophy books** — 哲学书
- **science fiction / sci-fi** — 科幻
- **thriller / mystery / detective novel** — 惊悚 / 推理 / 侦探小说
- **biography** — 传记 (a real person's life story); **autobiography** — 自传
- **self-help book** — 励志/自助类
- **fairy tale** — 童话
- **comic / graphic novel** — 漫画
- **poetry** — 诗歌
- **textbook** — 教科书

## Quick tips to hit band 8 in Part 1
- Answer in **2–3 sentences** — say your answer, then a quick *because / but / so*.
- Use **contractions** ("I'm", "it's", "I'd") — it sounds natural.
- Sprinkle in fillers like *"to be honest"*, *"actually"*, *"I'd say"* — they buy thinking time and boost fluency.
- **Don't memorise word-for-word** — examiners can tell. Learn the pattern, not the script.
