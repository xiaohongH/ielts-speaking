
Part 1 focuses on **fluency**. Answer quickly, keep it short and simple: give a direct answer, then one quick reason or example (2–3 sentences). Use contractions and natural fillers, and don't memorise word-for-word.

---

## 1. Work / Study *(almost always asked first)*

**Do you work or are you a student?**
> I'm working at the moment. I've got a job in e-commerce, and I've been doing it for about five years now.

**Do you like your job?**
> Yeah, I do actually. The pay's really good and I feel like I'm doing something meaningful — the only downside is that the workload's always pretty heavy.

**Why did you choose this job?**
> To be honest, I've wanted to be a product manager since university. I love the idea of creating something that could actually change the world.

**Would you like to change your job in the future?**
> Maybe one day. For now I'm happy where I am, but eventually I'd like something with a better work-life balance.

**You said you enjoy your job, but you also mentioned the workload is quite heavy. Isn't that a bit contradictory?**
> Ha, good point! But not really. I don't mind being busy — it gives me a sense of achievement. It's just about finding the right balance.

## 2. Hometown

**Where are you from?**
> I'm from a small town in the south of China. It's got beautiful scenery — lots of mountains and rivers — though it used to be quite a poor area.

**Do you like your hometown?**
> Not really, to be honest. I love the fresh air, but in such a small town everyone knows everyone, which feels a bit suffocating. So going back always leaves me feeling drained.

**Is it a good place for young people?**
> Not really, to be honest. There aren't many job opportunities, so a lot of young people move to bigger cities.

## Quick tips to hit band 8 in Part 1
- Answer in **2–3 sentences** — say your answer, then a quick *because / but / so*.
- Use **contractions** ("I'm", "it's", "I'd") — it sounds natural.
- Sprinkle in fillers like *"to be honest"*, *"actually"*, *"I'd say"* — they buy thinking time and boost fluency.
- **Don't memorise word-for-word** — examiners can tell. Learn the pattern, not the script.
